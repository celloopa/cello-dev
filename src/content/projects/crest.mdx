## Context

The first version was called Daybreak. It did its job, and then it started asking for upkeep. I had to commit the day in a morning ritual before the app was any use. Nothing happened unless the Mac app was open. Unfinished work rolled into tomorrow and sat there. One block was deferred six mornings in a row.

So I rebuilt it, and on October 29 the rebuild got its own name: Crest. Same goal, a calm plan for the day, but with new foundations: a planner that proposes and never nags, AI that can only suggest, and a look that holds up next to Apple's own apps.

## A look that follows the sun

Crest is paper by day and ink at dusk, and it switches at the real sunset. On the Now card a small blue sun crosses a horizon as the block runs.

The look took three tries. The first pass matched the design system and still looked wrong. The tokens and contrast checks all passed, but the dashed lines made it read like a wireframe. Round two, the one I switched to on October 15, drew each state with warm light and glass. On October 29 I dropped the glow. The canvas went flat: paper, ink, and one blue.

The name and the mark came the same day. Three sheets of marks went by and none of them stuck, so I asked for a planner with the sun coming up behind it. The mark is that closed planner, with a mountain shaped like Mount Hood cut into the cover. Code is still the source of truth. Figma is generated from the same tokens, and the tests fail if the two drift apart.

## Nothing is late

There's no ritual to unlock the day. Missed blocks wait in **Catch up**, one card at a time, each with choices worked out for you: later today, tomorrow, needs a time, done, or let go. On the iPhone, a check-in offers the same choices from the notification. The card's own words: nothing here is late, it just needs a place.

## AI that only proposes

The old agent got the whole app state, about 85k tokens, on every request. Now **Ask** and **Talk through** get a few hundred tokens of plan lines that code writes, and the model can only call propose tools. You say what's actually happening ("drop off first, then errands before work") and the plan comes back as a draft.

## Focus, everywhere

Start a block and it becomes a session. The ring counts down on the Now card and the lock screen, and a small panel floats over everything on the Mac and can hide itself from screen sharing. Going over time isn't a failure. It says "still going".

## Errands that plan their own route

Errands go into a bank. Pick a few and Crest makes a run: stops in order, a driving estimate from Apple Maps, and one block on the day. The estimate is marked "unverified", and one Undo takes the whole run back.

## Outcome and limits

I switched to the rebuild on October 15, two weeks before it was called Crest. It imported my old plans, all 70 days of them, and a second run created nothing. It runs on my Mac and iPhone with private iCloud sync, and other agents can change the plan only through a bridge where each change has an Undo and deletions wait for me.

Every feature started as a question I answered, and there have been more than a hundred so far. Each one was checked against sample data on both devices before it shipped. It's still a one-person product. Next is testing the planner with other people before I treat my own days as the rule.
