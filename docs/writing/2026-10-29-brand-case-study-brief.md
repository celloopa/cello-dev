# Brief for the brand audit and design-system agent

I’m splitting the Asheville Dispensary work on my cello-dev site into two connected stories. Keep this direction in mind while auditing and building, and collect the evidence as you go.

## The brand case study

The Asheville Dispensary case study should be about how I manage a family of brands and bring their work into a coherent system while preserving each brand’s identity. Asheville Dispensary, Plantbar and Nightshade should look like themselves. AltBevSolutions is a separate B2B beverage division; show its relationship when the evidence supports it.

The central idea is shared primitives, roles and reusable material structures, with each brand supplying its own styling. Explain where color, typography, spacing, hierarchy, logos, imagery and composition are shared, where they vary, and why. Don’t assume every value or layout should be identical. Keep “primitive,” “semantic role,” “component” and “template” distinct in the implementation, but explain their practical benefit in the case study.

Follow that system through the materials people actually need: social posts and stories, web and email banners, printables, signage, brand guides, decks, packaging and sales materials, across print and digital. A shared content structure can have different layout, type scale, density, safe area, bleed and export rules for each medium. Don’t imply a web component is automatically a print-ready template.

Show my role as the designer making decisions, reconciling existing sources, setting boundaries and helping people use the result. The case study should explain the judgment behind the work, rather than become a list of tools or agent outputs.

## The automation blog post

The separate post, working title “Automating product drops,” owns the manual flower workflow, the first automation, the time it freed up for motion design, Storybook exploration, the Familiar revisions, CEO feedback and the plan to use the new style for future drops.

The case study can link to that post as one example of what a coherent brand system makes possible. Give it a short connection, not a second telling of the pipeline, costs or time savings. The blog can link back to the case study for the wider brand architecture.

## Evidence to collect during the audit

For each meaningful decision, capture:
- The source artifact and date; a specific inconsistency or deliberate exception.
- What I decided, why, alternatives considered, and what stayed distinct.
- A matched before/after or side-by-side example; source file/frame/page links.
- The primitive/role/component/template relationship it demonstrates.
- At least one example of the same material structure adapted across brands, and one adapted across media.
- How it was checked: long names, dense copy, missing imagery, contrast, fonts, production/export requirements, or a real handoff.
- Approval, adoption and rollout status, with evidence. Separate existing production work, audited sources, proposed rules, editable prototypes, approved masters and published team templates.
- What people can actually do with it now, and what still needs implementation.

Keep an annotated contact sheet and a media manifest: filename, source, date, brand, medium, what the image proves, alt text, caption, permission/privacy status and current/proposed/legacy state. Preserve originals. Save readable overview shots plus useful close-ups of decisions and actual editable controls; include short interaction recordings where they show something a still cannot.

## The connections to think through

Treat the brand guides, Figma libraries, Storybook, documentation service and AD Hub as related surfaces with different jobs. Record the authority for brand decisions, where approved values live, who can approve changes, how versions reach templates and code, and how drift is detected. Distinguish an intended connection from one that has actually been implemented and tested.

Shared foundations should reduce duplicated decisions and make variation easier to govern. Find evidence for that claim; don’t invent time savings, adoption numbers, synchronization, or production readiness. Preserve purposeful brand, product and supplier exceptions. The design system should explain relationships rather than flatten the brands into a single look.

## Writing handoff

Build an evolving outline: the brand-management problem → my role → source audit → shared versus distinct decisions → cross-brand and cross-medium examples → validation and governance → current outcome and next rollout.

Keep facts, decisions, visuals, approval state and open questions linked together. Write in plain, first-person language when asked for prose. Mark anything unverified. Collect evidence now; don’t publish, alter the flower workflow, or overwrite another agent’s writing. Hand the evidence and case-study recommendations back to the cello-dev writing work.
