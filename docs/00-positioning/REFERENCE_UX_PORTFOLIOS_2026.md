# Reference: what UX hiring managers do with portfolios (2026)

Saved 2026-10-13 at Cello's request as input for the cello.dev rebuild. Summary in our own
words; numbers as published. Not yet acted on.

**Sources**
- LinkedIn post by David Pasztor (UXfolio): https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davidpasztor_the-numbers-are-in-we-talked-with-more-than-share-7515328608844947456-xo6P/
- Report landing page (full report gated behind email): https://uxfol.io/state-of-ux-portfolios-2026
- Survey of 100+ hiring decision-makers, mostly design leads and directors who review portfolios themselves. No fieldwork dates published. UXfolio sells a portfolio builder and announced a new one off the back of this survey, so read it as vendor research.

## Findings

| Topic | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| What decides fit | 78% lean on the portfolio and case studies most. Then industry experience 46%, CV 40%, design assessment 29%, past employers 12%. |
| First look | 65% spend under 4 minutes on first open. One respondent rejects if they can't find projects within ~30 seconds. One in ten reviewed 200+ portfolios last year. |
| Red flags | Low visual quality 55%, hard-to-navigate structure 48%, obvious AI overuse 43%, overly long or dense content 42%. |
| Format | 72% prefer a portfolio website (Figma prototype 30%, PDF 27%, slides 26%, Behance/Dribbble 14%). A Drive folder goes to the back of the pile. |
| Case studies | 89% say a *relevant* case study matters most; 64% say a clear account of thinking and key decisions makes one strong. |
| AI | Everyone has noticed AI in portfolios; 43% call overuse a red flag, most expect some use. Consensus: AI to build, not to think, and disclose where it was used. 71% say their org doesn't screen portfolios with AI. |

## Themes from the comments

- Tension between a sub-4-minute scan and wanting explained thinking: the answer is layering, a fast scannable surface (role, problem, key decisions) with depth on demand.
- Portfolio-first hiring disadvantages NDA work, non-visual work and career changers; senior hiring leans more on conversation.
- First-time portfolio sites take people 40+ hours to build.

## Why it matters for cello.dev

The rebuild is a full-screen layered landscape where content lives in depth. These numbers
put pressure on three things the Brain note already commits to: hiring managers reach
identity and work immediately; reading mode is calm and scannable; case studies lead with
decisions. Navigation clarity and time-to-first-project are the measurable risks. AI-built
site, human-made thinking, disclosed, fits the consensus and is a story worth telling.
